## Retrospective

While we kept our research question the same, we quickly realized that our initial method of keyword selection grew out of control quickly. Using a basic “set” of potential keyword vulnerabilities, we generated more than 6,600 results. Being able to use an “off the shelf” took care of how to isolate the keyword results. The addition of an established security scanner also helped with the defensibility of the results. Since we were using another artifact to scan with, we didn’t know how well it would work with the scope of the duplicate groups and same-name variants. We changed the unit of analysis from individual artifacts to baseline-variant pairs to capture modifications. A further reduction was based on the ideas that identical copies cannot add a capability, and very low similarity is likely a different skill. The reduction to 188 pairs worked great but should only be considered a starting point after reviewing the results. Modifications had to be made since most of the results were non-security related to modifications. The final results are a stepping stone for review of real vulnerabilities.
Security findings in the skill.md text of our sample set seem to be rare, so our artifact probably would need to be part of a bundle. Due to the number of missing commit dates, ensuring when changes are “introduced” is difficult. A much larger sample size may also generate more results. As of now, the sub-set of the same-name-variants csv is done by hand. We are looking to address some merge issues generated during creation that may be the cause. Next we plan to replicate results on bigger samples and try to dial in the variables that could undermine our results.
